\hypertarget{abstract}{%
\subsection{1. Abstract}\label{abstract}}

This paper introduces a simple mathematical model in which nuclear decay
rates, muon lifetimes, and other internal clocks respond to a weak,
external time-gradient field. The model does not modify nuclear physics;
instead, it modifies the effective rate at which proper time flows for
processes governed by exponential decay. The central equation is:

\textbackslash{[}\textbackslash lambda\_\{\textbackslash text\{eff\}\}(t)
= \textbackslash lambda\_0 \textbackslash left{[} 1 +
\textbackslash alpha \textbackslash, G(t)
\textbackslash right{]},\textbackslash{]}

where \textbackslash(\textbackslash lambda\_0\textbackslash) is the
standard decay constant,
\textbackslash(\textbackslash alpha\textbackslash) is a coupling
strength characteristic of each isotope or clock, and
\textbackslash(G(t)\textbackslash) is a dimensionless time-gradient
field. The corresponding effective half-life is:

\textbackslash{[}T\_\{1/2,\textbackslash text\{eff\}\}(t) =
\textbackslash frac\{\textbackslash ln 2\}\{\textbackslash lambda\_0 (1
+ \textbackslash alpha G(t))\} \textbackslash approx T\_\{1/2,0\}(1 -
\textbackslash alpha G(t)),\textbackslash{]}

valid for small \textbackslash(\textbackslash alpha G(t)\textbackslash),
as suggested by experimental data.

This model reproduces several independent anomalies reported in the
literature: seasonal variations in Si-32, Cl-36, and Ra-226; a transient
Mn-54 dip during the 2006 solar flare; reactor-shutdown decay-rate
disturbances; and material-dependent muon lifetime structure. Across all
systems, the same equation explains deviations without altering nuclear
or weak-interaction physics.

\hypertarget{introduction-evidence-for-time-dependent-decay-rates}{%
\subsection{2. Introduction: Evidence for Time-Dependent Decay
Rates}\label{introduction-evidence-for-time-dependent-decay-rates}}

Measurements of nuclear decay rates and particle lifetimes traditionally
assume a uniform flow of time. Under this assumption, the decay constant
\textbackslash(\textbackslash lambda\_0\textbackslash) and half-life
\textbackslash(T\_\{1/2,0\}\textbackslash) are intrinsic nuclear
properties, unaffected by external conditions except in extreme
environments. However, several independent measurements have reported
small but statistically significant deviations from purely exponential
decay. These include:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Seasonal oscillations in Si-32, Cl-36, and Ra-226.
\item
  A transient Mn-54 dip coincident with a solar flare.
\item
  Short-term anomalies during reactor shutdowns.
\end{itemize}

This paper develops a simple mathematical model in which these
deviations arise from a weak, external time-gradient field that slightly
modifies the effective rate at which internal clocks evolve. The model
introduces a multiplicative correction:

\textbackslash{[}\textbackslash lambda\_\{\textbackslash text\{eff\}\}(t)
= \textbackslash lambda\_0 (1 + \textbackslash alpha
G(t)).\textbackslash{]}

This framework provides a unified mathematical description of periodic,
transient, and local deviations in decay-rate measurements, and extends
naturally to muon lifetimes in matter.
