\section{Black Hole Lifecycle}\label{black-hole-lifecycle}}

Singularity elimination by the time-gradient field

\hypertarget{the-question}{%
\subsection{1. The question}\label{the-question}}

The foundation of this framework is a chain of three principles:
distance necessitates time, time is information, and information cannot
be lost. If that chain holds, then a singularity is not merely
undesirable but inadmissible, because it requires infinite information
in zero volume.

This paper asks what the field theory implies for black hole collapse.
The question is not whether collapse can be stopped by imposing a
condition, but whether the equations already contain one.

\subsection{2. Mass as extreme
time-density}\label{mass-as-extreme-time-density}}

seriously in a specific way. If mass is the terminal limit of
time-density, then the field that measures time-density must diverge

postulate; it is what the axiom chain requires.

\hypertarget{gravity-is-regulated-by-the-field}{%
\subsection{3. Gravity is regulated by the
field}\label{gravity-is-regulated-by-the-field}}

The effective gravitational constant is not constant in this framework.
It depends on the field, and regularizes as the field grows:

\textbackslash{[}G\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{eff\}\} =
G\_0\textbackslash left(1 - c\_g G\textbackslash right)\textbackslash{]}

The coupling is not free. MICROSCOPE bounds the fractional deviation of
the gravitational constant to below
\textbackslash(10\^{}\{-5\}\textbackslash). The framework writes that
deviation as \textbackslash(c\_g G\textbackslash), and with a background
field \textbackslash(G \textbackslash sim 10\^{}\{-3\}\textbackslash)
this requires

\textbackslash{[}c\_g \textless{}
\textbackslash frac\{10\^{}\{-5\}\}\{10\^{}\{-3\}\} =
10\^{}\{-2\}\textbackslash{]}

\textbackslash(c\_g G = 1\textbackslash). Substituting the field from

\hypertarget{the-bounce}{%
\subsection{5. The bounce}\label{the-bounce}}

Once \textbackslash(G\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{eff\}\} =
0\textbackslash) there is nothing left to compress the object further.
The field\textquotesingle s own stress is then decisive:

\textbackslash{[}\textbackslash rho\_G + 3P\_G =
2\textbackslash dot\{\textbackslash phi\}\^{}2 -
2V(\textbackslash phi)\textbackslash{]}

For a slowly rolling field,
\textbackslash(\textbackslash dot\{\textbackslash phi\}
\textbackslash to 0\textbackslash), and this reduces to
\textbackslash(-2V(\textbackslash phi) \textless{} 0\textbackslash) for
any non-zero potential. Negative \textbackslash(\textbackslash rho +
3p\textbackslash) is a repulsive, accelerated solution. The condition is
satisfied for every \textbackslash(V\_0 \textgreater{} 0\textbackslash)
and is therefore not a fine-tuning.

The collapse reverses and the envelope is ejected. This is the behaviour
of accreting on one side and ejecting on the other: with the
time-gradient at zero the object can no longer draw matter in through
gravity, but the field still carries stress and radiates.

\hypertarget{the-remnant}{%
\subsection{6. The remnant}\label{the-remnant}}

The collapse does not end in a singularity. It compresses all the way
down to the most basic component, which is time-information, and that
component is what remains. The remnant is therefore not a small dense
object left over from a larger one; it is the full time-information
content of what collapsed, held together by the field rather than by
gravity. Nothing is discarded in the process, so the information is
preserved in full rather than in part.

The content is the Bekenstein information at the freeze-out radius,
which for collapse from a mass \textbackslash(M\textbackslash) is

\textbackslash{[}I = \textbackslash frac\{2\textbackslash pi R
E\}\{\textbackslash hbar c \textbackslash ln 2\} =
\textbackslash frac\{4\textbackslash pi G M\^{}2\}\{\textbackslash hbar
c \textbackslash ln 2\}\textbackslash{]}

a classical expression used to size the content, not a relic mass. A
black hole of
\textbackslash(10\textbackslash,M\_\textbackslash odot\textbackslash)
holds \textbackslash(1.5\textbackslash times10\^{}\{79\}\textbackslash)
bits. This is the quantity the lifecycle preserves, and it is conserved:
at freeze-out the time-density the mass was supporting transfers to the
field, whose negative stress \textbackslash(\textbackslash rho\_G +
3P\_G \textless{} 0\textbackslash) then holds it, while the mass-energy
departs in the ejecta.

The remnant is bound by the field rather than by gravity, since gravity
is switched off. Balancing the residual gravitational pull against the
field\textquotesingle s own repulsion,

\textbackslash{[}\textbackslash frac\{GM\}\{r\^{}2\} =
\textbackslash frac\{8\textbackslash pi
G\}\{3\}\textbackslash frac\{r\textbackslash,V(\textbackslash phi)\}\{c\^{}2\}\textbackslash{]}

and substituting \textbackslash(M =
\textbackslash tfrac\{4\}\{3\}\textbackslash pi r\^{}3
\textbackslash rho\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{rem\}\}\textbackslash)
gives a clean bound on the remnant density,

\textbackslash{[}\textbackslash boxed\{\textbackslash;\textbackslash rho\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{rem\}\}
=
\textbackslash frac\{2V(\textbackslash phi)\}\{c\^{}2\}\textbackslash;\}\textbackslash{]}

Using the potential fixed by the DESI energy split (\textbackslash(w =
-0.85\textbackslash), so \textbackslash(K/V = 0.0811\textbackslash),
giving \textbackslash(V\_0 =
0.7\textbackslash,\textbackslash rho\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{crit\}\}\textbackslash)):

\textbackslash{[}\textbackslash rho\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{rem\}\} =
1.19 \textbackslash times 10\^{}\{-26\} \textbackslash{}
\textbackslash mathrm\{kg\textbackslash,m\^{}\{-3\}\} =
1.40\textbackslash{}
\textbackslash rho\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{crit\}\}\textbackslash{]}

The remnant sits at \emph{cosmic} density, not nuclear density. It is a
field-stabilised region, not a compact object, and it carries no
Schwarzschild charge because it never crossed a horizon. Its binding
length is the field Compton wavelength,
\textbackslash(\textbackslash lambda\_C =
\textbackslash hbar/(m\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{eff\}\}
c)\textbackslash).

\textbf{Open input: the relic abundance.}

The mechanism above fixes the remnant\textquotesingle s binding length
and the density it can support. It does \emph{not} fix how many remnants
exist. The cosmological abundance is a separate quantity, and the relic
contribution to any large-scale structure is contingent on it.

No relic mass is asserted here, and none is refuted. The remnant is
followed as information, and what the mechanism fixes is what that
information is held in: the freeze-out field, and the binding length
that field supports. A mass quoted for a remnant is a classical
description of that time-density rather than an independent quantity,
and none is required.

Empirical data narrow this further, though not to a single answer. The
evaporation horizon, the mass whose Hawking lifetime equals the age of
the universe, is

\textbackslash{[}M\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{eh\}\} = (3 B
t\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{univ\}\})\^{}\{1/3\} = 1.73
\textbackslash times 10\^{}\{14\}\textbackslash{}
\textbackslash mathrm\{g\} = 7.95 \textbackslash times
10\^{}\{18\}\textbackslash,M\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{Pl\}\}\textbackslash{]}

Compact objects below that mass emit the remainder of their mass as
radiation, and that emission has been searched for directly. Voyager~1
and INTEGRAL limit any such population to under
\textbackslash(0.1\textbackslash\%\textbackslash) of dark matter;
AMS-02, with diffusive reacceleration extending the sensitivity, reaches
\textbackslash(10\textbackslash\%\textbackslash). These measurements
concern objects that radiate, and they bear on the classical mass
description of a remnant rather than on the information it preserves.

A mass of
\textbackslash(10\^{}\{-5\}\textbackslash,M\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{Pl\}\}
= 2.18\textbackslash times10\^{}\{-10\}\textbackslash{}
\textbackslash mathrm\{g\}\textbackslash) has been carried elsewhere in
this body of work. Read as a classical mass it lies
\textbackslash(7.9\textbackslash times10\^{}\{23\}\textbackslash) below
the evaporation horizon, inside the range those instruments exclude, and
a population of radiating objects at that mass is excluded to better
than one part in a thousand. Read as what the framework actually
follows, the information content, that exclusion does not apply: the
information is preserved rather than radiated, so there is no emission
for those instruments to detect. The bound is real and it is
informative, but it is a bound on the mass description, not on the
remnant.

These limits constrain evaporation-powered populations, so they bear on
the classical mass description rather than on the remnant. The
attribution of
\textbackslash(\textbackslash Omega\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{relic\}\}
= 0.27\textbackslash) to radiating objects at the quoted mass fails by a
factor of 270, which is a statement about that description and not a
constraint on what freeze-out preserves. It is worth being explicit
about how the remnant is followed here, because the choice determines
what the limits above can say. A black hole is followed as information,
not as classical mass. Its conserved content is the Bekenstein
information at the horizon,

\textbackslash{[}I = \textbackslash frac\{2\textbackslash pi R
E\}\{\textbackslash hbar c \textbackslash ln 2\} =
\textbackslash frac\{4\textbackslash pi G M\^{}2\}\{\textbackslash hbar
c \textbackslash ln 2\}\textbackslash{]}

which is
\textbackslash(1.5\textbackslash times10\^{}\{79\}\textbackslash) bits
for a
\textbackslash(10\textbackslash,M\_\textbackslash odot\textbackslash)
hole. Freeze-out preserves that content. The time-density the mass was
supporting transfers to the field, whose negative stress
\textbackslash(\textbackslash rho\_G + 3P\_G \textless{}
0\textbackslash) then holds it; the mass-energy departs in the ejecta.
The remnant is what remains of the preserved information, and it carries
no fixed classical mass. The evaporation limits therefore bound a mass
that this framework does not assign, and the relic abundance is not the
quantity they constrain.

The clustered energy is the field\textquotesingle s. The field carries
\textbackslash(\textbackslash rho\_F\textbackslash), set to
\textbackslash(0.7\textbackslash,\textbackslash rho\_\{\textbackslash mathrm\{crit\}\}\textbackslash)
by the expansion data, and it is the field that clusters under its own
gradient. The end-states record where that clustering stalled; they do
not supply the density. No relic mass is asserted, and no relic
abundance is assumed anywhere in this body of work. Values quoted above
are transcribed in \texttt{evaporation\_constraints.txt} and evaluated
by \texttt{relic\_abundance.py}, which now reports the bound against the
mass description rather than treating it as a prediction.

A note on method. Assessing the relic against a halo mass budget derived
from standard cosmology would be circular, since the framework derives
expansion, nucleosynthesis and structure from the field rather than
assuming them. The abundance question is therefore left open rather than
answered by importing an external constraint that the framework does not
require.

\hypertarget{conclusion}{%
\subsection{8. Conclusion}\label{conclusion}}

Collapse does not need to be stopped. The equations already stop it.

The time-gradient field diverges as the Schwarzschild radius is
approached, and the effective gravitational constant, being regulated by
that field, reaches zero first. The freeze-out radius is
\textbackslash((1+\textbackslash sqrt\{1+c\_g\^{}2\})/2 \textbackslash,
r\_s\textbackslash), which lies outside the horizon for every positive
coupling. The field\textquotesingle s own stress is repulsive, so the
collapse reverses rather than terminating in a singularity. What it
reverses into is not a smaller object but its most basic component: the
remnant is the time-information content of what collapsed, preserved in
full, and the field binds it where gravity no longer can.

Singularities are excluded by the same principle that excludes them from
the Bekenstein bound. Information cannot be lost, and an event horizon
would require it to be.
