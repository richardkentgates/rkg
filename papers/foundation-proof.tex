\begin{abstract}
Three principles form the foundation of existence: (1) distance necessitates time, (2) time is information, (3) information cannot be lost. We prove these mathematically using the metric, the Bekenstein bound~\cite{Bekenstein1973}, and unitarity~\cite{tHooft1993,Susskind1995}. We show that singularities violate all three principles and are therefore forbidden. The scalar field $G(t)$ is the mathematical expression of this prohibition: it prevents collapse to infinite density (black holes)~\cite{Hawking1976} and prevents expansion from infinite density (Big Bang). Accelerator physics provides direct evidence for the distance-time-information coupling through Lorentz lifetime dilation~\cite{Lorentz1904}, energy-time uncertainty~\cite{Heisenberg1927,Mandelshtam1945}, and cross-section modifications by $G(t)$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Foundation}

Three principles form the foundation of existence:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Distance necessitates time.} Spatial separation requires temporal separation. A signal crossing distance $\Delta x$ requires time $\Delta t = \Delta x/c$.
  \item \textbf{Time is information.} A clock tick is a state change. Each state change is one bit. The Bekenstein bound~\cite{Bekenstein1973} gives $\sim 9$ bits per Planck time.
  \item \textbf{Information cannot be lost.} Unitarity~\cite{tHooft1993,Susskind1995} requires information preservation. The Bekenstein bound sets the maximum information content of any region.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Distance Necessitates Time}

From the metric $ds^2 = -c^2 dt^2 + dx^2$:
\begin{align}
\text{Lightlike: } dx &= c\,dt \\
\text{Timelike: } dt &> dx/c
\end{align}

At the Planck scale: $l_{\text{Pl}} = c \cdot t_{\text{Pl}}$. Distance IS time at the fundamental level.

\section{Time Is Information}

The Bekenstein bound~\cite{Bekenstein1973} at the Planck scale:
\begin{equation}
I_{\text{max}} = \frac{2\pi R E}{\hbar c \ln 2} = \frac{2\pi}{\ln 2} \approx 9.06 \text{ bits per Planck time}
\end{equation}

For the observable universe (holographic bound):
\begin{equation}
I_{\text{univ}} \sim \left(\frac{R}{l_{\text{Pl}}}\right)^2 \sim 10^{122} \text{ bits}
\end{equation}

\section{Information Cannot Be Lost}

A singularity ($r \to 0$, $\rho \to \infty$) would require infinite information in zero volume. This violates the Bekenstein bound~\cite{Bekenstein1973}. Therefore singularities are forbidden~\cite{Penrose1965}.

The scalar field $G(t)$ enforces this:
\begin{equation}
G \to \gamma^{-1} \text{ as collapse proceeds} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{the time gradient weakens} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{collapse stops}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
M_{\text{relic}} > 0, \quad R_{\text{relic}} > 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{distance} > 0, \text{ time} > 0, \text{ information preserved}
\end{equation}

\section{The Information-Distance-Time Triangle}

Distance requires time (signal propagation). Time is information (state changes = bits). Information requires distance (Bekenstein bound: $I \sim RE$). All three are fundamentally coupled.

\end{equation}

Higher energy probes shorter distances and shorter times. Each measurement carries at least 1 bit.
